# Writing corporate & business documents

Business writing exists to **inform a decision or drive an action**. The reader is
usually senior, busy, and reading between meetings. Your job is to let them grasp
the point — and what you need from them — in seconds, then find supporting detail
only if they want it.

## Lead with the bottom line (BLUF)

Put the main point, recommendation, or ask **first** — in the subject line and the
opening sentence. This is the single highest-leverage habit in business writing.
A reader who stops after one line should still know what you're telling them and
what happens next.

- Weak open: *"Over the past quarter, the team has been evaluating several
  options for our data platform, and after much analysis…"*
- Strong open: *"Recommendation: migrate to Platform B by Q3. It cuts cost 30% and
  removes our biggest outage risk. Details below."*

## Structure supporting detail as a pyramid

After the headline point, group your support into a few labeled buckets, each
leading with its own mini-conclusion, then evidence beneath. This "answer first,
then grouped reasons" shape (the Minto pyramid) lets readers descend only as far
as they need. Order sections by importance to the reader, not chronology.

## Make it actionable

Every business document should make the next step unmistakable. For any request,
state:

- **What** needs to happen,
- **Who** owns it,
- **By when.**

"Someone should look into this eventually" is not an action. "Priya to approve the
budget by Friday" is.

## Document shapes

**Memo / update**
1. Subject line = the takeaway, not the topic.
2. First line: the point or ask (BLUF).
3. 2-4 grouped supporting points, each with a lead sentence.
4. Explicit next steps (what / who / when).

**Executive summary** (stands alone; a reader should act on it without the full report)
1. Purpose — the decision or problem, in one or two sentences.
2. Key findings — the 3-5 most important, ordered by significance.
3. Recommendation — clear and direct.
4. Only the most relevant evidence for each point. Keep it to a page or so.

**Proposal / business case**
1. The recommendation and the outcome it delivers, up front.
2. The problem or opportunity, sized with real numbers.
3. Options considered, and why this one.
4. Cost, risks, and how you'll mitigate them — don't hide these; addressing them
   builds credibility.
5. The ask and timeline.

**Business email**
- Subject line carries the point or action ("Approval needed: Q3 budget by Fri").
- First sentence states why you're writing and what you need.
- Keep it short; if it runs long, it probably wants a summary at the top and detail
  below, or an attachment.

## Tone

Aim for **professional and direct**, not stiff or bureaucratic. Plain words and the
active voice read as confident and competent, not casual. Avoid corporate filler
("synergize," "circle back," "boil the ocean," "at the end of the day") — it adds
length and signals nothing. Be warm and human where it fits; you're writing to a
person, not a committee.

## Self-check for business writing

- Could a reader act correctly after reading only the first two lines?
- Is the ask explicit, with an owner and a date?
- Are risks and costs stated honestly rather than buried?
- Have you removed corporate jargon and hedging?
- Is it as short as it can be while still standing on its own?

## Before / after

- Before: *"We wanted to reach out to touch base regarding the ongoing situation
  with the vendor, as there are a number of factors that may potentially need to be
  taken into consideration going forward."*
- After: *"We need to decide whether to renew the vendor contract. It's up March 31.
  I recommend we don't — here's why."*

The rewrite names the decision, the deadline, and the recommendation in three
sentences. The reader can act immediately.
