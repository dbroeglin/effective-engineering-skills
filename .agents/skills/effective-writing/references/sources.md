# Sources & further reading

This skill is an **original synthesis** of widely-accepted, publicly-available
writing guidance. It captures *principles and methods* — which are not protected by
copyright — expressed in our own words. It deliberately contains **no verbatim
quotations, tables, or extended passages** from any copyrighted book. Where an idea
is associated with a particular author or work, we credit it here rather than
reproduce their text.

## Public-domain sources

- **U.S. Federal Plain Language Guidelines** and the **Plain Writing Act of 2010**
  (plainlanguage.gov / digital.gov). U.S. Government works — public domain. Source
  of the plain-language core: write for your audience, put the main idea first, use
  short sections and sentences, the active voice, common words, and lists/tables.
- **William Strunk Jr., *The Elements of Style* (1918 edition).** Public domain.
  Source of the enduring maxims to omit needless words and prefer the active voice.

## Openly-licensed sources

- **GOV.UK / Government Digital Service style guidance** — Crown copyright, published
  under the Open Government Licence (reuse permitted with attribution). Reinforces
  plain English, short words, the active voice, one idea per sentence, and writing
  for everyone.
- **Diátaxis** (Daniele Procida, diataxis.fr) — a freely published framework for
  organizing documentation into tutorials, how-to guides, reference, and explanation.
  We reference the framework's structure and credit it as such.
- **Google developer documentation style guide** — published under CC BY 4.0.
  Corroborates second-person address, the imperative for instructions, and present
  tense in technical writing.

## Referenced concepts from copyrighted works

We use the *ideas* below and attribute them; we do not reproduce the authors' text.
Readers who want the full treatment should go to the original works.

- **Josh Bernoff, *Writing Without Bullshit* (2016).** The stance that you should
  treat the reader's time as more valuable than your own, lead with the point, and
  cut fluff, jargon, and hedging.
- **Barbara Minto, *The Minto Pyramid Principle*.** Structuring an argument as an
  answer first, with grouped supporting reasons beneath.
- **William Zinsser, *On Writing Well*.** Clarity, simplicity, and ruthless cutting
  in nonfiction.
- **BLUF (Bottom Line Up Front)** — a U.S. military and business convention for
  putting the conclusion first. **Inverted pyramid** — a journalism convention for
  ordering information by decreasing importance.
